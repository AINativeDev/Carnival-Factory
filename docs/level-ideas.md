# Level ideas

A level is a whole patch of the North Pole of your own: a little world laid
out how you like, with its own squares, that visitors reach by sleigh from the
sleigh station by the North Pole's entrance. The factory builds what you
describe, so a little detail goes a long way. Borrow freely from anything here.

## What a level is made of

- **Ground:** snow everywhere, with paths, ice, water and wooden bridges where
  you want them.
- **Props** from the North Pole's own catalogue: pines, snowdrifts, log cabins,
  lamps, fences, the reindeer stable, sleighs, a big decorated tree, striped
  tents, benches, cocoa carts, banners, bunting, and one frozen pond that
  skaters go round.
- **Squares of its own,** up to 8: little animated scenes on stalls, each drawn
  like a booth, that visitors walk into.
- **Where visitors arrive,** and a sleigh back to the North Pole.
- **A theme** from the North Pole's palette: dusk, aurora night, candy cane,
  frozen lake or gingerbread.

Everything else is drawn by the North Pole itself, so a level always looks at
home, and the factory checks that every square can be walked to.

## What makes a good request

Say **what the place is, what's in it, and how it should feel**. Compare:

> A cool winter level with lots of stuff.

with:

> A frozen-lake fair. A big pond in the middle with skaters going round, a
> skate rental hut on one side and a cocoa hut on the other, and an
> ice-fishing hole on the shore. Lamps along the paths, pines round the edge,
> a little stream with a bridge. Cosy and busy, at dusk.

The first leaves every choice to the agent. The second gets close on the first
try, and tells the factory how to check it's done.

A few things that help:

- **One place.** A fair, a village, a forest clearing, a harbour. Small is
  good: a level is at most 41 tiles each way, and a cosy one is about 21.
- **Its landmarks.** The two or three things people should find: a pond, a
  big tree, a stable. Say roughly where they go: in the middle, by the
  entrance, along the back.
- **Its squares.** Up to 8, each one idea, the way a booth is. Say what each
  one shows and what moves in it.
- **What moves.** Skaters on the pond, lamps that glow, the stream; inside
  each square, a loop that repeats every few seconds.
- **Little details.** A banner, a bench by the water, a snowdrift by a cabin.
- **Your colours,** from the palette, and a theme.

## Ideas to start from

- **Frozen-lake fair:** a pond with skaters, a skate rental, a cocoa hut, ice
  fishing. (This is the starter level, `carnival/levels/jedi4ever/`.)
- **Reindeer ranch:** the stable, fenced paddocks, a feed cart, and squares
  for a reindeer school, a harness shop and a flight-test runway.
- **Gingerbread village:** a lane of cabins, a big tree, and squares for a
  bakery, a candy-cane mill and an icing workshop.
- **Aurora camp:** tents round a clearing under the northern lights, pines,
  and squares for a stargazing telescope, a campfire and a lantern maker.
- **Toy harbour:** water with bridges, a dock of sleighs, and squares for a
  boat builder and a lighthouse keeper.

## What a level can't do

A level is data the North Pole draws, so it has no code of its own: no sound,
no links, nothing from the internet, and nothing outside its own folder. A
prop that isn't in the catalogue can be one of its squares instead, drawn on
its stall. If part of your idea can't be built, the factory proposes the
closest version it can and asks you first.
