# Booth ideas

Your booth is a small animated scene, about the size of a stall window, drawn
in the North Pole's colours. The factory builds what you describe, so a
little detail goes a long way. Borrow freely from anything here.

## What makes a good request

Say **what's in the scene, what it's doing, and how it should feel**. Compare:

> Something Christmassy with animals.

with:

> A frozen pond at dusk. Two penguins in red scarves skate slow figure eights,
> and a third wobbles at the edge holding a mug of cocoa that steams. Pine
> trees behind, a lantern that flickers. Cosy and a little silly.

The first leaves every choice to the agent. The second gets close on the first
try, and tells the factory how to check it's done.

A few things that help:

- **One idea.** A pond, a shop, a workshop, a tiny scene. Not a whole town.
  For a whole world of your own, ask for a [level](level-ideas.md).
- **What moves, as a loop.** Booths animate from time, so describe something
  that repeats every few seconds: a skater's circuit, smoke rising, lights
  twinkling in turn.
- **What happens when someone points at it.** A booth can react to the mouse
  or a finger over it: a penguin that waves when you hover, a snowball you
  throw with a tap, a little game. On the map a click still takes people inside
  the booth, so taps and games are for in there.
- **A focal point.** One thing the eye lands on first, then the small details.
- **Little touches.** A sign with a pun, a cat on a roof, a snowman with
  sunglasses. These are what people remember.
- **Your style.** By default a booth is drawn in the
  [diorama style](style.md), like the rest of the North Pole: a little
  isometric scene on a slab of snow at dusk, lit warm from the upper left, so
  it sits naturally on its stall. Or pick another in the form: cosy storybook,
  bold poster, papercut, pixel art, tiny model railway, or a glowing night
  scene. The factory keeps it in the dusk light and the North Pole's palette.

## Your stall

Your drawing goes on the front of your stall on the map. The stall itself can
be one of a few shapes the map knows how to draw, with its roof in one of the
North Pole's colours:

- **Market stall**, the usual one: wooden walls, a counter and a striped
  awning.
- **Shed**: long and low, with a flat roof.
- **Tent**: round, with a striped canvas roof and a pennant on top.
- **Cabin**: a log cabin with a snowy pitched roof.
- **Kiosk**: a small hexagonal kiosk with a pointed roof.

Pick one under **Stall shape** in the form, and name a roof colour, and a
second colour for the stripes or the awning if you like, in your request: "a
shed with a gold roof", "a tent in pine green and cream". The colours are the
palette's: snow, ice, pine, berry, gold, wood, cocoa, gingerbread, cream,
violet, mint, aurora and the rest of the `theme`. Your sign and door stay where
they are, so people find you in the same place.

## What a booth can't do

A booth only draws on its own square, so it can't play sound, load pictures or
fonts from the internet, link anywhere, or store anything. It only knows
where the pointer is over its own square, never the rest of the page. It also
can't flash fast, which isn't safe for everyone. Keep it friendly
for a family audience at work, and make it your own: no logos or characters
someone else owns, though anything _in the spirit of_ one is fine.

## Ideas to start from

### Cosy

- A reading nook by a fireplace, with a cat curled on a footstool whose tail
  twitches, and snow falling past the window.
- A tiny bakery: gingerbread coming out of the oven on a tray, steam curling
  up, a robin on the windowsill.
- A hot spring in the snow with three snow monkeys, one of them wearing a
  little towel on its head.

### Busy

- An elf post office: letters drop down a chute, get stamped, and slide into
  sacks on a conveyor.
- A reindeer flight school: one reindeer takes off over and over, wobbles, and
  lands in a snowdrift.
- A toy repair shop where a wind-up robot gets a new key and marches off.

### Night and lights

- A lighthouse on an iceberg, its beam sweeping slowly through falling snow.
- A cabin under the northern lights, with the aurora rippling across the sky
  and one window glowing gold.
- A lantern festival on a frozen lake, with lanterns rising slowly and drifting
  off the top.

### Silly

- A snowman barber shop: a snowman gets his carrot nose trimmed.
- Penguins queuing for an ice cream van in a blizzard.
- A polar bear trying to fit into a tiny igloo, giving up, and building a
  bigger one.

### Made for you

- Your hometown's best-known building, covered in snow.
- Your team's mascot, ice skating.
- Your favourite hobby, North Pole style: a snowy climbing wall, a cabin
  recording studio, a kitchen full of elves.

## Changing your booth later

Open **Your booth** again and describe what should be different: "Add a third
penguin", "Make it night time", "Give the snowman a scarf", "Make my stall a
cabin with a berry roof". Your latest request
is the one that counts.

## Games

A booth can be a little game. Inside the booth the drawing fills the back
wall, and it knows where the mouse or a finger is and every tap on it, so
people can play right there. Keep a round short, and show the score in the
scene.

- **Snap against the elves.** Cards flip onto a pile; tap when two match before
  the elf across the table slaps it first.
- **Catch the snowflakes.** Move a mitten or a mug left and right to catch
  flakes falling at different speeds.
- **Ring the bells in order.** Bells light up in a tune, then you tap them back
  in the same order, one note longer each round.
- **Feed the reindeer.** Tap a carrot to toss it to whichever reindeer pokes its
  head out of the stable.
- **Whack-a-snowman.** Snowmen pop up from drifts; tap one to send its hat
  flying before it ducks down again.

A game counts every tap, even two quick ones between frames, and its score
lasts for the visit: it starts again next time, since a booth can't store
anything.
