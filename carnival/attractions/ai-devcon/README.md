# AI DevCon

An attraction of the North Pole: it belongs to the map, not to a participant.
The billboard by the road opens it.

A game you play in a keynote hall at dusk, seen from the back of the room: a
lighting truss with stage lights sweeping slowly through the haze, a big
screen over the stage, an elf speaker at a lectern, warm footlights, and four
rows of attendees seen from behind, in elf hats, beanies and headphones, their
laptops glowing past their shoulders. A banner on the left holds the score,
the best score of the visit and the hype meter; one on the right says when and
where AI DevCon is next: London, 7–8 June 2027, The Brewery.

## Keep the room buzzing

Tap to play, and the visitor gets their ATTENDEE badge, dropping in on its
lanyard. Then attendees put their hands up round the hall, each with a
question in a bubble and a bar that runs down while they wait.

- Tap a question about building software with AI (agents, specs, skills,
  evals, context, tests) to pass its asker the mic. The mic flies to them from
  its stand in the aisle, they jump up cheering, and the clap and the glow of
  everyone's badges spread out from there. A quick answer scores more, and the
  hype meter fills.
- Leave the off-topic ones be, like "Where's the coffee?". Tapping one drains
  the hype, and so does a good question left waiting too long. The hype ebbs
  on its own too.

The screen shows the talk on now. Each good question adds one of its points to
the slide, and every five the hall claps the next speaker on: six talks in
all. As the keynote goes on, questions come quicker, wait less and stray off
topic more often. The talks are examples of the topics, not the real
programme, and name nobody.

Answer the whole keynote for a standing ovation: the hall rises with its hands
in the air, and a gold SUPERFAN badge drops in. Let the hype run out and the
room goes quiet, heads down. Either way the screen shows the score and the
best, the conference's dates and a big **Get tickets** button; tap anywhere
else to play again.

With nobody playing, a demo passes the mic to the good questions on its own and
scores nothing, while the screen says how to play.

## Tickets

The **Get tickets** sign beside the stage, and the button on the end card, ask
the page to open the panel's first link, the conference's tickets, in a new
tab. The drawing itself links nowhere: its `ask()` answers `{ open: 0 }` once,
on the frame after the tap.
